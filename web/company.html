How we measure narrative risk

The idea. Narrative economics (Robert Shiller) shows that stories spread through a population like an epidemic and, past a tipping point, change what people buy. Quantara measures that for one industry, brand by brand, as it happens.

What we read. US food news, recalls (FDA, USDA), Reddit, Bluesky, YouTube and Google searches for "boycott / cancel / alternative". A story only counts if it is about the US food business and something real is at stake: prices, safety, workers, a boycott or trust.

How a story is scored. Reach (how many outlets), regions, severity, momentum (new articles), and how fast it fades once coverage stops. We also track its spread rate: new coverage in the last day compared with the day before. Above 1 means each wave is bigger than the last.

What players add. Players spot trouble before the news, bet on how fights end and post evidence. Their calls feed the outlook: the early warning before the index itself moves.

How we check ourselves. Every warning is written down the moment it happens and later compared with what really followed: public attention to the brand (Wikipedia readers) and its share price against the S&P 500. See the track record below.
